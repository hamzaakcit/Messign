# ADR-014: Journey results compare treatment with the holdout on mature customers, with a 95% interval, computed from the ledger on each request

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-10, WP-21)
- **Context:** The pilot has to prove revenue uplift against a holdout (D-11: 10% of eligible customers get no message; the product vision measures "revenue per enrolled customer within the attribution window, treated vs holdout"). Two ways to get that wrong are common: comparing groups before the attribution window has closed (the groups then differ in how much time they had), and reporting a difference without its uncertainty (a 1-point lift on 100 customers is noise). Results also have to reconcile with the tables they come from, or nobody trusts them.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Conversion** is at least one `order_completed` for the customer in the window after they entered the journey (`[entered_at, entered_at + window]`, inclusive), and not after the evaluation moment. Revenue is the sum of the orders' `total` (unreadable totals count as zero). The window is 24, 72 (default) or 168 hours; the attribution windows of the product vision (72 h for cart, 7 days for win-back) are the defaults for the two flagship journeys.
  2. **Maturity:** a customer counts only when their window has ended. A customer still inside the window is shown in the enrolment counts but not in the rates, so neither group is favoured by having had more or less time.
  3. **Uplift** is treatment minus holdout, in percentage points, with a 95% interval from the normal approximation for a difference of two proportions (`±1.96·√(p₁(1−p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1−p₂)/n₂)`). It is "significant" only when the interval excludes zero. The relative uplift is shown only when the holdout rate is above zero.
  4. **No cache, no materialised summary:** each request recomputes from `engage.enrollments`, `engage.enrollment_steps`, `engage.messages` and `engage.events`. A stored aggregate would drift from its source the moment a late event arrives.
  5. **Tests check the reconciliation, not just the query:** an independent computation reads the enrollments and orders one by one and must match the counts, conversions and revenue for three window lengths and for orders on both sides of the window and the evaluation moment. The formula is tested against hand-computed values, including the zero-interval and empty-group cases.
- **Consequences:** + results can be checked by hand against the database; + a customer cannot be counted before their window closes; + the interval says when the data cannot yet tell the groups apart. − a normal approximation is rough for very small groups or rates near 0 or 1 (the pilot's groups are in the hundreds to thousands; a Wilson or exact interval is the next step if a result depends on a small group); − results are computed per request, which is fine for one journey's enrolments and needs a summary table at tens of millions of events.
- **Not in v1:** per-step funnel charts (the counts per step exist in the ledger; the page shows the message counts), attribution by channel, multiple comparisons across many journeys, a sequential test that stops early.

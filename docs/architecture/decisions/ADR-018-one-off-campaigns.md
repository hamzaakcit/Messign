# ADR-018: A one-off campaign is a one-step journey plus a record; it sends to customers with consent, only when sending is ready, and only once per request

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-10, WP-38)
- **Context:** Engage could send email only as a step of an activated journey. A person who wants to send one email to a group now (a new-release mail, an announcement) had no way to do it, and asked for it repeatedly. A second, parallel send path would have to rebuild what a journey already has: the send gate on every message, the holdout, the results, the customer trail, and the record of who approved what. A bulk send also needs care a journey step does not: it can send to many customers at once, and it must not queue messages for a tenant that cannot send yet (the gate holds them without a decision, and they would all go at once the day sending is switched on).
- **Decision:**
  1. **A campaign is a journey.** `POST /api/engage/campaigns` creates a journey with trigger `api`, no exit events, no re-entry and one message step (purpose `marketing`, the template version pinned), approves and activates it as the admin who pressed Send, and enrolls the audience directly. Nothing in the definition schema changed. Every message therefore goes through the send gate (consent, suppression, quiet hours, frequency cap, sandbox allow-list, budget, content), and the results (WP-21), the customer trail and the audit of who approved and activated work unchanged.
  2. **A record per campaign** (`engage.campaigns`, migration 127, forced RLS): the template and segment versions pinned at send time, the holdout, the idempotency key, the status (`sending`, `sent`, `failed`), the counts (matched = enrolled + skipped), a cursor, the cap flag and who started it. The engage role can update progress columns only: a finished campaign and what it was asked to do cannot change, and nothing can be deleted. The journey of a campaign is shown with its campaign, not in the journey list.
  3. **The audience is the segment's customers that have email consent.** Customers without it are not enrolled and not messaged, so they do not appear as blocked either. The gate still checks consent on every message.
  4. **Readiness is checked first, and a refusal writes nothing.** Sending must be on (`SEND_MODE`), the tenant's `send_enabled` set, exactly one active email account whose mode matches the send mode, an email rate card and a monthly budget cap (the gate blocks every message without them), the public https origins the unsubscribe links are built from (without them no marketing message can be created), and, in live mode, the legal readiness items recorded. A refusal answers 409 with every reason. The preview and `GET /campaigns/readiness` report the same facts.
  5. **One request, one campaign.** The idempotency key decides the journey key, so a double click, a retry after a timeout, or two simultaneous requests return the same campaign (200 for a repeat, 201 for the first) and send nothing twice.
  6. **The audience is enrolled in chunks** (500, at most 100,000 per campaign, like a scheduled segment run), each chunk one transaction that holds the campaign row and the cursor, so an interrupted send is finished by `POST /campaigns/:id/resume` with exact counts and nobody enrolled twice. Stopping the campaign's journey in the middle ends the send where it is (`failed`, `journey_stopped`).
  7. **Admins only** start and resume; any tenant user reads. The wizard needs a check of the audience, the readiness and an explicit confirmation before it sends; the template must have an unsubscribe link (the gate would block every message without it).
  8. **Holdout defaults to 0.** It is optional (0 to 50%): a one-off send is not a measurement unless the sender asks for one.
- **Consequences:** + sending one email to a segment is possible without a second send path, with everything a journey has; + a campaign cannot be sent twice, cannot be queued for a tenant that cannot send, and cannot reach a customer without consent; + an interrupted send can be finished with exact counts. − the Journeys list hides campaign journeys, so the Campaigns list is where they are; − a campaign cannot be scheduled for later or cancelled once sent (a stopped journey ends the send, but sent mail is sent); − the readiness items are fixed by an operator until the settings screen (WP-22) exists.
- **Not in v1:** scheduling for later, A/B variants, a test send to oneself, an approval by a second person (D-13: self-approval is allowed in the pilot), AI-written copy (WP-23/24).

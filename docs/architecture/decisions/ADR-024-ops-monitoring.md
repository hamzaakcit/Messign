# ADR-024: The ops check watches every tenant's delivery from the ledgers we already keep, tells the operators in one digest, and the restore is proven by a check, not by hope

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-23, WP-29)
- **Context:** Before real customers, we need to know when sending breaks without waiting for a customer to tell us. The live checks found how silent failures are: a rotated account broke the webhook URL (no delivery reports, nothing logged as an error), and delivery reports only arrived once the app was subscribed to the WhatsApp Business Account. Nothing watched gate denials, failing providers, jobs that died, or whether a backup restores.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Measure from the ledgers.** Every five minutes (pg-boss job `engage/ops-check`) the check finds the tenants that can be sending (`engage.ops_work`, ids only, like the other work lists), then measures each in its own tenant transaction under row-level security: provider calls in the last 15 minutes (`message_events` from the dispatcher: sent, failed, retry scheduled, with error codes), what the provider reported back in the last 24 hours (bounces, complaints, any report at all), messages accepted but never reported on, gate denials by code against the previous day's usual, messages waiting past their time, stuck leases, the trigger outbox, and the tenant's own attention items (email domain lost, İYS sync). Nothing is written to take a measurement. Counts, rates, codes and ids only.
  2. **Rules are a pure function** (`ops/rules.ts`) of the measurements and the thresholds (`ops/thresholds.ts`, our defaults, overridable with `ENGAGE_OPS_*` variables, none of them a provider's rule). Rules: `provider_failing` (25% warning, 50% critical, over at least 10 calls), `credentials_rejected` (3 refusals, critical, overrides the rate), `delivery_reports_missing` (5 or more messages accepted over 30 minutes ago and no provider report at all in 24 hours, for providers whose adapter declares delivery reports; this is the rotated-URL and unsubscribed-app failure), `bounce_rate_high` (5% / 10% of at least 50 email in 24 hours), `complaint_rate_high` (0.1% / 0.5% of at least 500, at least 2 complaints), `gate_denials_systemic` (5 / 50 in the window, for codes that mean the setup is wrong: gate_error, sender_identity_mismatch, no_rate_card, budget_exceeded and the like), `gate_denials_spike` (ordinary reasons such as no consent only when at least 50 and 5 times the usual), `messages_stuck` (judged only while sending is on for the tenant and SEND_MODE is not off), `trigger_outbox_stuck`, and the two attention items.
  3. **Alerts have a life** (`engage.ops_alerts`, migration 136, per tenant with the normal RLS policy; codes and numbers only): opened, refreshed while the condition holds, escalated from warning to critical, reminded (critical every 6 hours, warning every 24), resolved after the condition has been gone 10 minutes. A condition flickering around its threshold opens one alert. The tenant's admin sees their own open alerts under "Needs attention" on the Settings screen and each account's health on a "Delivery health" card (`GET /settings/delivery-health`, computed live with the same rules).
  4. **The operators are told in one digest per run** (`ops/notify.ts`): a JSON POST to `ENGAGE_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` (`text` for Slack-style and `content` for Discord-style hooks, plus structured `notices`). The URL is a secret and is never logged or stored; http is accepted only for loopback. A refused or unreachable webhook leaves alerts "not told yet", so the next run tells them. An optional `ENGAGE_OPS_HEARTBEAT_URL` is pinged after each clean run for an external dead-man service, which is what tells us if the ops check itself stops.
  5. **Platform checks** (no tenant): the dead-letter depth of the engage queues (pg-boss jobs in state `failed` in the last 24 hours, read with the server's pool), told on appearance, reminded, and told when it clears. Held in memory: a restart tells a lasting problem once more.
  6. **Error tracking, in the process, no third party.** Errors caught in the engage job handlers and everything engage code logs with `logError` are grouped by a fingerprint (source and kind, with ids and numbers taken out), counted, logged at 1, 10, 100…, and told to the operators the first time a group appears and when it bursts (20 in 10 minutes, at most hourly). Only the redacted description is kept: no stack, no request, no data. History is the server log. A hosted tracker (for example Sentry in an EU region) is decision D-17.
  7. **The restore is proven by a check** (`ops/restoreCheck.ts`, `npm run engage:restore-check`): on a restored database it verifies the schema against the migration files, recorded migrations, forced RLS on every engage table, the application role (roles are not in a dump), the deriving functions and triggers, every message's status against its last applied event, every consent state against the ledger, the current month's events partition, and, with a manifest taken from the live database, that no table has more rows than live and how far behind the restore point is. Read-only; it must run as a role that sees every row.
- **Consequences:** + a failing provider, rejected credentials, a lost webhook, a setup mistake that blocks sends, a dead job and a lost domain each show up within minutes, with the operators told once; + the same rules drive the operators' digest and what the tenant sees; + no new send path and nothing that can fail a send: the check only reads ledgers and writes alerts; - the ops check is one job: if it stops, only the heartbeat tells us (set it); - the error groups and platform alert state live in one process; - the thresholds are our first guesses and need tuning with the first real traffic.
- **Not in v1:** paging or on-call routing (one chat channel), per-tenant alert preferences, SMS provider health (no SMS adapter yet; the rules are provider-agnostic and apply once one exists), metrics dashboards, and a restore drill against the production host (an operator step: `docs/runbooks/ops-monitoring.md`).
- **Evidence:** `engageOpsUnit.test.ts` (rules, thresholds, notices, error grouping), `engageOps.integration.test.ts` (the drill on real Postgres and HTTP: real failures through the WhatsApp mock open, tell once, keep and resolve alerts; a real HTTP webhook receives the digest; a down webhook is retried; tenant isolation; the Settings routes; the restore check passes and catches tampering), the isolation test (`ops_work` is ids only, `ops_alerts` is tenant-bound). A restore drill with `pg_dump` and `pg_restore` of a 470 MB local database (1.0M consents, 2.3M events) passed every check (2026-11-23, local PostgreSQL 17).
